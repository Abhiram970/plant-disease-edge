\begin{abstract}
Cloud vision--language models diagnose plant disease accurately but cost tens of cents per image
and need
connectivity, suiting neither smallholder nor in-field use. The harder obstacle is that a classifier
cannot name a disease on a crop absent from its training set. We ask how small a deployable model can
be while still performing cross-crop zero-shot diagnosis, matching a leaf image to symptom
descriptions written by a large language model and grounded in cited sources. Using the SAGE
crop-disease dataset and frozen compact contrastive language--image pre-training encoders, we report
four results. First, frozen encoders from 11.4 to 86.3 million parameters diagnose unseen crops
at 3.5--12.6 times chance, averaged over four tiers, across nested held-out sets of 16, 34 and 51
classes; accuracy is nearly flat over that range. Second, and centrally, only source-grounded descriptors scale: as the
unseen label space triples, hand-curated symptom text degrades from 28.0\% to 20.4\% while
source-grounded text improves from 21.7\% to 24.7\%, so auditability is not a tax but the mechanism
that reaches new crops at all. Third, an abstain gate makes top-1 field-useful: grounded top-5 is
61--77\% on 16 unseen classes and 58--76\% on 51. Fourth, the encoder runs at 17.4 ms per
image on a laptop processor in a 12.9 MB 8-bit-integer footprint, though 8-bit quantisation proves a
size rather than a speed lever for hybrid encoders. On known crops the same backbone reaches 82.4\%
over 166 classes. Fourteen supervised baselines are stronger on seen crops yet
structurally incapable of cross-crop transfer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

%% ACTION: the per-image cost below needs a citation or must be removed. It is currently
%% unsourced and it is the sentence carrying the paper's motivation.
Cloud VLM diagnosers achieve high in-the-wild accuracy but cost of the order of tens of cents
per image and
require connectivity --- a poor fit for smallholder and field use, which needs offline, low-cost,
real-time inference. A second, deeper obstacle is generalisation: models trained on one crop transfer
poorly to others~\citep{bouacida2025}, and edge deployment additionally demands compression. Cross-crop
transfer is
therefore the foundation-model-native open problem this Special Issue targets: it is enabled only by an
image--text-aligned VLM, not by a conventional classifier.

We study the question: \emph{how small can a model be and still perform cross-crop disease zero-shot at
the edge?} Our system is one \textbf{frozen} compact Contrastive Language--Image Pre-training (CLIP)
encoder with two heads and an abstain router: a trained head for accurate real-time diagnosis on known
crops, and a descriptor head that diagnoses unseen crops by matching image embeddings to
large-language-model (LLM) authored, source-grounded symptom-descriptor text.
Only the image encoder ships to the device; text prototypes are precomputed offline.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Frozen compact VLMs do cross-crop zero-shot.} Averaged over the four deployable
tiers, they diagnose unseen crops at 3.5--12.6$\times$ chance across 16-, 34- and 51-class held-out
sets (the 11.4\,M tier alone spans 2.9--11.3$\times$), and accuracy is flat from 11\,M
to 300\,M --- model size is not the bottleneck (Sections~\ref{sec:flat}--\ref{sec:desc}).
\item \textbf{A nested scale study showing that only source-grounded descriptors scale.} Detail is the
first lever (+15.2 points over class names at 16 classes), but as the unseen label space triples,
hand-curated descriptors \emph{degrade} while LLM source-grounded ones \emph{improve}, overtaking them
by 4.3 points at 51 classes. Auditability is therefore not a cost but the only authoring route that
reaches new crops (Section~\ref{sec:desc}).
\item \textbf{A margin-ranked abstain gate plus top-5} makes the modest top-1 field-useful
(Section~\ref{sec:abstain}).
\item \textbf{A per-tier edge benchmark} with real INT8 sizes and CPU latency, and a quantisation
result with practical reach: INT8 helps transformer encoders but \emph{hurts} hybrid
convolution--transformer encoders, with a graph-level diagnosis and a deployment rule
(Section~\ref{sec:int8}).
\item \textbf{Rigorous negatives that save effort:} naive fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting,
a supervised CNN cannot transfer cross-crop, and a leave-one-crop-out analysis shows the held-out crops
are not cherry-picked.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Agricultural VLMs} SAGE~\citep{arshad2026sage} provides our data and the cloud agent we contrast
against; its
\{value, source\_url, verbatim\_quote\} source-grounded schema and its reported 14--16 point gain from
symptom knowledge motivate our descriptor design. We are the deployable edge counterpart at \$0/image.

\paragraph{Descriptor-based classification} Descriptor-based CLIP classification
(DCLIP)~\citep{menon2023} and Customized Prompts via Language models (CuPL)~\citep{pratt2023} classify
by LLM-written visual descriptions. In contrast, our descriptors are source-grounded and therefore auditable,
compressed to the edge, and evaluated for \emph{cross-crop} agricultural transfer rather than general
retrieval.

\paragraph{Compact CLIPs} CLIP~\citep{radford2021} established image--text contrastive pretraining;
MobileCLIP~\citep{vasu2024}, its successor MobileCLIP2~\citep{mobileclip2} and
TinyCLIP~\citep{wu2023} compress it, and provide our frozen backbones. We use the
open\_clip~\citep{ilharco2021} implementations throughout, with SigLIP\,2~\citep{siglip2}, whose
sigmoid objective follows \citet{zhai2023}, as a reference ceiling. We contribute an agricultural cross-crop zero-shot study and an
edge efficiency analysis rather than a retrieval benchmark.

\paragraph{Robust fine-tuning} WiSE-FT \citep{wortsman2022} explains our forgetting result and
motivates the frozen-backbone plus weight-ensembling design.

\paragraph{Domain foundation models} We evaluate the biological foundation model
BioCLIP\,2~\citep{bioclip2} and the leaf-disease model SCOLD~\citep{scold2025} as candidate backbones
and find they do not transfer under our descriptor protocol (Section~\ref{sec:bakeoff}).

\paragraph{Vision-only foundation models} Self-supervised encoders such as
DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024} learn strong visual features but have no text tower, so they cannot match an
image against a written symptom description. Zero-shot diagnosis of an unseen crop therefore requires
an image--text-aligned model by construction, which is why every backbone we evaluate is
CLIP-family.

\paragraph{Supervised baselines} Our comparison set spans ResNet~\citep{he2016},
MobileNetV3~\citep{howard2019} and MobileNetV4~\citep{qin2024} among others
(Section~\ref{sec:baselines}).

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Dataset and the nested design}
\label{sec:data}

We use SAGE~\citep{arshad2026sage} only, drawing images from the released
dataset~\citep{sagedata2026} --- $\sim$839\,K images over 335 crops and 1{,}251 disease classes,
released under the MIT licence --- to avoid the
``old, lab-only'' critique of legacy corpora such as PlantVillage~\citep{mohanty2016}, on which
in-distribution accuracy has long been saturated. We stream-filter shards to our crops with a
per-class cap and content-hash
de-duplication.

To test whether the approach \emph{scales} rather than reporting a single convenient split, we define
three \textbf{nested} configurations --- every seen and held-out crop in A also appears in B, and B in
C --- so differences are attributable to the size of the label space and not to which crops were
chosen. Configuration A holds out Coffee, Orange and Peach (16 classes); B adds Cotton, Wheat and Bean
(34); C adds Banana and Cucumber (51). Seen-set sizes are given in Table~\ref{tab:seen}. Held-out crops
are never used for training at any stage; they are diagnosed only from descriptor text.

\subsection{Source-grounded descriptors}

Each disease carries a symptom record with fields for pathogen, affected organs and visual symptoms,
each field carrying a value, a source URL and a verbatim quote. Records are generated by an LLM under a
strict grounding prompt that forbids un-cited claims, then audited. Because the generation endpoint
cannot browse, raw citations are model-recalled; we therefore web-verify the headline held-out
diseases, replacing citations with sentences copied verbatim from reachable authoritative pages. The
symptom text --- the string that becomes the CLIP text prototype --- is the functional component; the
verified citations provide the auditability guarantee. Records that could not be grounded are stored as
explicit stubs and are excluded by the loader, so a placeholder can never become a prototype.

\subsection{Architecture}

One frozen image--text encoder per tier, plus: (1)~a trained seen-crop linear probe; (2)~a zero-shot
descriptor head selecting the nearest source-grounded text prototype by cosine similarity;
(3)~WiSE-FT weight ensembling, interpolating fine-tuned and frozen visual weights at ratio $\alpha$;
and (4)~an abstain router on the top-1 minus top-2 similarity margin. Only the image encoder deploys.
We evaluate four deployable tiers (11.4, 21.5, 35.8 and 86.3\,M image-encoder parameters) plus a
92.9\,M reference ceiling.

\subsection{Descriptor strategies}

\emph{bare} (class name only) $\rightarrow$ \emph{crude} (one generic keyword sentence) $\rightarrow$
\emph{rich} (a hand-curated symptom paragraph) $\rightarrow$ \emph{grounded} (the LLM source-grounded
symptom text, falling back to \emph{rich} where a record is absent).

\section{Results}

\subsection{A frozen 11\,M model already does cross-crop zero-shot, and accuracy is flat with size}
\label{sec:flat}

Across the nested splits (Protocol N) the four deployable encoders span only 17.0--24.2\% (rich)
and 22.2--29.1\% (grounded) at configuration C despite a 7.6-fold spread in parameters, and the efficiency curve is
essentially flat from 11\,M to 300\,M (Fig.~\ref{fig:eff}). Model size is not the bottleneck; the
pretrained alignment is the asset. Attempts to \emph{train} a small model to learn this alignment fail
--- on the earlier 17-class pilot set (Protocol P, not comparable with the nested splits above)
a 5.5\,M student distilled from scratch reaches only 11.0\% against 5.9\% chance, versus 19.9\%
for the frozen 11.4\,M encoder it was distilled from; and specialising that encoder on seen crops
loses 4.5 points of unseen
accuracy to catastrophic forgetting (Fig.~\ref{fig:forget}) --- so we keep the backbone frozen.

\subsection{Encoder bake-off}
\label{sec:bakeoff}

Fig.~\ref{fig:bakeoff} summarises the comparison. The 11.4\,M encoder recovers 86\% of the 92.9\,M reference at one eighth the parameters, and
\emph{beats} the larger 21.5\,M model --- a training-recipe effect that reinforces that pretraining
quality, not size, drives transfer. Biological and domain leaf-disease foundation models perform at or
below chance under a descriptor protocol: taxonomy-style contrastive pretraining does not align to
symptom-descriptor text. We note openly that our wrapper for one domain model is a best-effort load and
a faithful evaluation would need the authors' inference pipeline; we do not rest any claim on it.

\subsection{Descriptor detail is the lever --- and only source-grounded descriptors scale}
\label{sec:desc}

Table~\ref{tab:scale} and Figs.~\ref{fig:descscale} and~\ref{fig:descabl} carry the paper's central result.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_descriptor_scaling.png}
\caption{Mean zero-shot accuracy over the four deployable encoders as the unseen label space grows.
Hand-curated descriptors degrade; source-grounded descriptors improve and overtake them.}
\label{fig:descscale}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Detail is the first lever} Any full symptom description beats a class-name prompt. At the
focused scale A the gain is 15.2 points on average, closely matching the 14--16 points that SAGE
reports for symptom knowledge in a cloud setting, here reproduced on frozen 11--86\,M edge encoders.

\paragraph{Hand-curation does not scale; source-grounding does} As the unseen label space grows from 16
to 51 classes, hand-curated descriptors degrade monotonically (28.0\%, 24.3\%, 20.4\%) while LLM
source-grounded descriptors improve (21.7\%, 23.5\%, 24.7\%). At configuration C, grounded beats rich
for \emph{all four} encoders (mean $+4.3$ points) and is the only strategy that still clearly beats a
bare class name. The mechanism is coverage rather than cleverness: the hand-written symptom bank was
authored against the three anchor crops and thins out as new crops enter, whereas the generated
registry covers 156 of 217 classes under a uniform schema.

This inverts the conclusion available from a single split. Source-grounding is not merely an
auditability tax paid for equal accuracy --- it is what makes the descriptor approach extend to new
crops at all, which is precisely the capability this paper claims. Measured against chance, the
grounded head becomes \emph{relatively stronger} as the problem gets harder, from 3.5$\times$ to
12.6$\times$.

\subsection{Top-5 and a margin-ranked abstain gate}
\label{sec:abstain}

A field tool can propose a short list and abstain when unsure, so raw top-1 is not the operative
metric. Table~\ref{tab:abstain} and Fig.~\ref{fig:riskcov} show grounded top-5 at 61--77\% on 16 classes, holding at 58--76\% on
51 classes (29--38$\times$ chance). Selective accuracy rises monotonically as coverage tightens,
confirming the confidence signal is correctly ordered rather than inverted. Across models the
grounded strategy also abstains best, holding the lowest area under the risk--coverage curve and the
highest top-5 even where its top-1 does not lead --- making it the recommended deployment setting.

\subsection{Known crops, and the seen/unseen dial}

The same frozen backbone with a linear probe reaches 82.4\% over 166 fine-grained classes
(Table~\ref{tab:seen}, Fig.~\ref{fig:seenscale}), against 9.3\% for the same encoder used zero-shot on its own seen classes.
Two patterns repeat here. Accuracy is flat across the same 7.6-fold parameter range ($\leq$1.5 points at
every scale), mirroring Section~\ref{sec:flat}; and accuracy \emph{rises} as the seen label space grows
(+3.3 to +3.5 points from 97 to 166 classes, for every encoder), the opposite of the unseen side,
because additional crops contribute proportionally more training images than decision difficulty. Each
head is therefore deployed where its scaling behaviour is favourable. WiSE-FT (Table~\ref{tab:wiseft}, Fig.~\ref{fig:wiseft})
turns the balance into one tunable knob: $\alpha=0.5$ buys 5.1 points of seen accuracy for only 0.7
points of unseen, whereas naive fine-tuning nearly halves unseen accuracy.

\subsection{Supervised baselines and leave-one-crop-out}
\label{sec:baselines}

Fourteen supervised baselines --- twelve convolutional networks and two hybrid
convolution--transformer models --- spanning 1.7--42.8\,M parameters, all on the identical 166-class seen
set for 8 epochs (Table~\ref{tab:cnn}; per-epoch curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:cnntrain}), are strong on known crops: the best reaches 88.6\%, about
6.2 points above the frozen probe. We report this plainly, because for a fixed, known label set a
conventional classifier is the better choice.

Capacity is not the lever, and here the evidence is blunt: the best architecture has 4.4\,M parameters
while the largest, ResNet-101 at 42.8\,M, is 1.8 points \emph{worse}; across a 25$\times$ range the
family spans 6.3 points with no monotone trend. That reproduces Section~\ref{sec:flat} and the seen
probe above in a third, fully supervised setting, making ``size is not the bottleneck'' a property of
the task rather than an artefact of frozen backbones. The sharpest control is FastViT-SA12 (10.7\,M),
the same architecture family as our image encoder (11.4\,M) at nearly the same size: 87.4\% trained
supervised against 82.4\% as a frozen probe, so the 5.0-point difference is training regime, not
architecture. Yet none of the fourteen has an output unit for an unseen class, so cross-crop accuracy
is not merely low --- it is structurally undefined at any parameter count.

Table~\ref{tab:loco} shows the held-out crops fall in the middle of the per-crop range, with two
\emph{trained} crops the hardest, so the headline held-out set is not a favourable split.

\subsection{INT8 is architecture-dependent, not universally beneficial}
\label{sec:int8}

A naive dynamic-quantisation call --- the default recipe in most tutorials --- makes the lightweight
tiers 18--19$\times$ \emph{slower}. A properly configured static quantise--dequantise (QDQ) pipeline
(shape-inference
pre-pass, per-channel weights, calibrated activations) recovers 5--9$\times$ of that but still does not
beat FP32 on those tiers. The pure-transformer model behaves oppositely and becomes 2.2$\times$ faster.

The mechanism is visible in the quantised graphs: counting convolutions the quantiser could not
convert gives 119, 215 and 235 for the three hybrid tiers against 3 for the pure transformer. On the
hybrids the runtime must dequantise, run a float convolution, and requantise hundreds of times per
inference; dynamic quantisation instead maps every convolution to an integer-convolution operator that
is pathologically slow for depthwise kernels on x86.

\paragraph{Practical guidance} For hybrid convolution--transformer encoders on a CPU runtime, INT8 is a
\emph{size} lever, not a \emph{speed} lever; deploy FP32. For transformer encoders it is both. We flag
rather than assume that Arm processors, whose vector extension provides well-optimised INT8 depthwise
kernels, may reverse this for the hybrid tiers.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The contribution is a system, not a backbone} The tiers are off-the-shelf encoders used
frozen; the novelty is the source-grounded descriptor head, the margin-ranked abstain gate, the WiSE-FT
knob, and the real-time edge packaging. Because accuracy is flat with size, the reason to offer a
family is the measured latency/size Pareto, not accuracy.

\paragraph{Source-grounding is a trust contribution and, at scale, an accuracy one} On a focused
three-crop set hand-curation wins, which is why a single-split study would conclude that auditability
is a free but optional extra. The nested design shows the opposite. Hand-curation does not scale
because it cannot --- it requires an expert per crop, whereas a grounded generator requires a source
per crop. We consider this the paper's most transferable claim beyond agriculture.

\paragraph{Honesty on absolutes} Fine-grained cross-crop top-1 is modest (17--29\%); we lead with top-5
and the margin-ranked abstain gate as the field-relevant metrics, and frame cross-crop transfer as a hard
open problem we advance at edge scale rather than claim to solve.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Descriptor coverage and two tiers of citation} Across 18 crops, 156 of 217 disease records
are LLM-filled; the remaining 61 are explicit stubs that fall back to the hand-curated bank. Of the
156, 111 carry a verbatim quote but only 16 have been page-verified, meaning the quote was copied from
a page we retrieved and read. The other 95 quotes are model-recalled and must be treated as unverified
provenance, not evidence.

\paragraph{The grounding prompt over-refuses} Because the generation endpoint cannot browse, a strict
``never cite what you cannot quote'' instruction makes the model return empty records rather than risk
fabrication, including for well-documented diseases. A bulk re-run converted only 1 of 66 stubs. This
is the anti-hallucination guarantee behaving correctly, but it means grounded coverage scales with
human-supplied sources, not with more API spend.

\paragraph{Label noise in the dataset} Several held-out ``diseases'' are not diseases: three are
breeding resistance ratings, for which no symptom descriptor can exist; one is an insect pest rather
than a pathogen; and one is a misspelling of a genus. We recommend excluding the rating labels and
report this as a dataset-quality finding, since label noise of this kind directly inflates the apparent
class count of any fine-grained benchmark.

\paragraph{Scope and variance} Configuration C covers 18 of the dataset's crops, not all of it. Results
use a single random seed on a single evaluation machine; apart from the leave-one-crop-out bootstrap
intervals we report no variance estimates. As a partial check, configuration C re-measured three weeks
apart on an independently rebuilt embedding cache reproduced to within 0.15 percentage points.

\paragraph{Sub-10\,M gap} No off-the-shelf aligned model exists below about 11\,M parameters; a
$\sim$5\,M tier would require weight-inherited distillation and is framed as future work.

\section{Conclusion}

A single frozen, compact, descriptor-driven VLM brings cross-crop plant-disease diagnosis to laptops
and small devices at \$0/image: cross-crop zero-shot at 3.5--12.6$\times$ chance, averaged over the
four deployable tiers, on 16--51 unseen
classes, 82.4\% (87.7\% with WiSE-FT) on 166 known classes, grounded top-5 of 61--77\% at 16 unseen classes and 58--76\% at 51, a
margin-ranked abstain gate,
and 17.4\,ms real-time CPU inference in a 12.9\,MB INT8 footprint. Model size is a near-non-factor across the
11.4--304\,M range probed in Fig.~\ref{fig:eff}, and across the 11.4--86.3\,M deployable family in
every other experiment. The levers are, in order: the descriptor authoring method --- and
specifically the finding that only source-grounded descriptors keep improving as the unseen label space
grows --- honest abstention, and the frozen-backbone hybrid.
