\begin{abstract}
Cloud vision--language models diagnose plant disease accurately but bill per query and need
connectivity; a conventional classifier has no output unit for an unseen crop. We ask how small a
deployable model can be and still diagnose unseen crops, by matching a leaf image to LLM-written,
source-cited symptom descriptions. On SAGE, with frozen compact contrastive language--image
encoders: first, encoders from 11.4 to 86.3\,M parameters diagnose unseen crops at 1.5--5.7 times
the majority-class prior (3.4--12.2 times uniform chance) across nested held-out sets of 16, 34 and
51 classes. Second, which authoring wins depends on scale: a keyword bank leads at 16 and 34 unseen
classes (28.9\%, 25.0\%) but degrades to 21.5\% at 51, where source-grounded text reaches 23.9\%;
a de-duplicated re-run confirms the bank's decay but not grounded's rise, and the bank's prototypes
collide, so that gap measures distinctness, not sourcing. A matched ungrounded control over seven
seeds finds no measurable cost to requiring a citable source: $+0.94$ points over all 51 classes
and $+1.96$ on the 41 grounded filled, a subset its own refusals define; both intervals include
zero, a null rather than equivalence. Third, grounded top-5 reaches 60--77\% on 16 unseen classes
(31.3\% random) and 59--75\% on 51 (9.8\% random), far above top-1. Fourth, the smallest encoder runs at
17.4\,ms/image on a laptop CPU at 45.8\,MB, and reaches 82.2\% over 166 known classes. Read the
shortlist, not the top-1; pick the tier by objective, smallest for latency and footprint, largest
quantised for shortlist accuracy.
\end{abstract}

Cloud VLM diagnosers achieve high in-the-wild accuracy but are metered per query and
require connectivity: a poor fit for smallholder and field use, which needs offline, low-cost,
real-time inference. A second, deeper obstacle is generalisation: models trained on one crop transfer
poorly to others~\citep{bouacida2025}, and edge deployment additionally demands compression. Cross-crop
transfer is
therefore the foundation-model-native open problem this Special Issue targets: it is enabled only by an
image--text-aligned VLM, not by a conventional classifier.

We study the question: \emph{how small can a model be and still perform cross-crop disease zero-shot at
the edge?} Our system is one \textbf{frozen} compact Contrastive Language--Image Pre-training (CLIP)
encoder with two heads and an abstain router: a trained head for accurate real-time diagnosis on known
crops, and a descriptor head that diagnoses unseen crops by matching image embeddings to
large-language-model (LLM) authored, source-grounded symptom-descriptor text.
Only the image encoder ships to the device; text prototypes are precomputed offline.

recognition problem~\citep{scheirer2013openset,xian2019gzsl} that we do not solve here, and we

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Agricultural VLMs} SAGE~\citep{arshad2026sage} provides our data and the cloud agent we contrast
against; its
\{value, source\_url, verbatim\_quote\} source-grounded schema and its reported 14--16 point gain from
symptom knowledge motivate our descriptor design. We are the deployable edge counterpart: inference
costs nothing per image once the descriptor registry has been authored, which is a one-time cost in
generation tokens and human verification rather than a per-query one. The wider agricultural
foundation-model landscape is surveyed by \citet{chen2024foundation}, whose taxonomy frames
the gap we target: adaptation and deployment cost rather than raw capability. Domain-adapted
contrastive models have since appeared. AgriCLIP~\citep{nawaz2024agriclip} aligns a CLIP
backbone to agriculture and livestock imagery, and SCOLD~\citep{scold2025} targets leaf
disease specifically, but both are cloud-scale, and we evaluate the latter as a candidate
backbone in Section~\ref{sec:bakeoff}. \citet{lu2025vlmready} report that zero-shot
vision--language models still underperform supervised baselines on plant disease by a wide
margin, which is consistent with the modest absolute top-1 we report and is why we lead with
shortlisting and abstention rather than raw top-1. Our position differs from the compression
literature in kind: we keep an off-the-shelf encoder frozen rather than distilling a student,
which removes the student-training step and its accompanying reproducibility burden.

\paragraph{Descriptor-based classification} Descriptor-based CLIP classification
(DCLIP)~\citep{menon2023} and Customized Prompts via Language models (CuPL)~\citep{pratt2023} classify
by LLM-written visual descriptions. In contrast, our descriptors are source-grounded and therefore auditable,
compressed to the edge, and evaluated for \emph{cross-crop} agricultural transfer rather than general
retrieval.

\paragraph{Compact CLIPs} CLIP~\citep{radford2021} established image--text contrastive pretraining;
MobileCLIP~\citep{vasu2024}, its successor MobileCLIP2~\citep{mobileclip2} and
TinyCLIP~\citep{wu2023} compress it, and provide our frozen backbones. We use the
open\_clip~\citep{ilharco2021} implementations throughout, with SigLIP\,2~\citep{siglip2}, whose
sigmoid objective follows \citet{zhai2023}, as a reference ceiling. We contribute an agricultural cross-crop zero-shot study and an
edge efficiency analysis rather than a retrieval benchmark.

\paragraph{Robust fine-tuning} WiSE-FT \citep{wortsman2022} explains our forgetting result and
motivates the frozen-backbone plus weight-ensembling design.

\paragraph{Domain foundation models} We evaluate the biological foundation model
BioCLIP\,2~\citep{bioclip2} as a candidate backbone and find it does not transfer competitively under
our descriptor protocol, reaching 1.6$\times$ chance against 4.6$\times$ for a general-purpose
11.4\,M encoder. We also attempted the leaf-disease model SCOLD~\citep{scold2025}, but only through a
best-effort wrapper, so we report its number without resting any claim on it
(Section~\ref{sec:bakeoff}).

\paragraph{Vision-only foundation models} Self-supervised encoders such as
DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024} learn strong visual features but have no text tower, so they cannot match an
image against a written symptom description. Zero-shot diagnosis of an unseen crop therefore requires
an image--text-aligned model by construction, which is why every backbone we evaluate is
CLIP-family.

\paragraph{Selective prediction and open-set recognition} Deferring on low-confidence inputs is
selective prediction, formalised by \citet{elyaniv2010selective} and carried to deep networks by
\citet{geifman2017selective}; our margin-ranked gate is an instance of it, evaluated on the
risk--coverage curve those works define. Deciding whether an input belongs to the trained label set
at all is open-set recognition~\citep{scheirer2013openset}, and the seen/unseen split we assume
given is the generalised zero-shot setting surveyed by \citet{xian2019gzsl}. We adopt the metrics
of the first and leave the second unsolved, which Section~\ref{sec:limits} states as a gap.

\paragraph{Supervised baselines} Our comparison set spans ResNet~\citep{he2016},
MobileNetV3~\citep{howard2019} and MobileNetV4~\citep{qin2024} among others
(Section~\ref{sec:baselines}).

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Dataset and the nested design}
\label{sec:data}

We use SAGE~\citep{arshad2026sage} only, drawing images from the released
dataset~\citep{sagedata2026} ($\sim$839\,K images over 335 crops and 1{,}251 disease classes,
released under the MIT licence) to avoid the
``old, lab-only'' critique of legacy corpora such as PlantVillage~\citep{mohanty2016}, on which

%% per-class cap", which is not reproducible: a reader cannot rebuild this subset from the
%% description. Values verified against config.py (CAP_HELD_PER_CLASS=600,
%% CAP_TRAIN_PER_CLASS=1000, EVAL_MIN_CLASS_IMAGES=25) and against the build receipt
%% dataset_summary.json. Selection is prepare_upload.py: sorted(files)[:cap].
in-distribution accuracy has long been saturated. We stream-filter shards to our crops with
content-hash de-duplication, storing images at a longest edge of 288 pixels since every model
here operates at 224. Classes are capped at 600 images for held-out crops and 1{,}000 for seen
crops, and a class needs at least 25 images to enter the study at all; images are taken in sorted
filename order up to the cap. Filenames in the released dataset are content hashes, so this order
is uncorrelated with capture time or any other property of the image: the cap acts as a
deterministic random sample rather than favouring, say, the earliest-collected photographs, and
the subset is exactly rebuildable from the released code. The caps bound class imbalance rather than save disk: they are what keep the majority-class
baseline at 4.2\% on the largest held-out configuration, so a frequency prior cannot masquerade as
transfer. Nine of the 51 held-out classes reach the 600-image cap, so the held-out set is a
capped sample of the pinned revision rather than the whole of it. The resulting subset is 84{,}123
images over 217 classes and 18 crops: 14{,}204 images in the 51 held-out classes and 69{,}919 in
the 166 seen classes.

We pin the dataset to revision \texttt{bc9bd2899f} (7 May 2026). This is not a formality: SAGE was
re-released on 24 August 2026 with re-canonicalised crop and disease names, and the later release
drops Cotton entirely: its own canonicalisation record marks every Cotton entry as having no
canonical crop. Evaluating against the later release would therefore yield seven held-out crops and
48 classes at our largest configuration rather than the eight and 51 reported here. Every number in
this article refers to the pinned revision, and the fetch code pins the same commit hash.

To test whether the approach \emph{scales} rather than reporting a single convenient split, we define
three \textbf{nested} configurations: every seen and held-out crop in A also appears in B, and B in
C. Configuration A holds out Coffee, Orange and Peach (16 classes); B adds Cotton, Wheat and Bean
(34); C adds Banana and Cucumber (51). Seen-set sizes are given in Table~\ref{tab:seen}. Held-out crops
are never used for training at any stage; they are diagnosed only from descriptor text.

Nesting means no configuration drops a crop that an earlier one contained, so the comparison is
not confounded by crops disappearing. It does not hold difficulty constant: each step also
introduces new crops with their own images, and Table~\ref{tab:loco} shows per-crop zero-shot
accuracy spanning 4.8\% to 28.9\% on a common pool. Movement from A to C therefore mixes a
widening label space with a changing crop mix, and we do not attribute it to label-space size
alone. The clean control is to score one fixed image set against label spaces of increasing size,
which requires no new data and no new descriptors; we name it in Section~\ref{sec:limits} as the
experiment that would separate the two, and read the A-to-C columns as a description of three
operating points rather than as a controlled scaling curve.

Two evaluation protocols appear in this article and are never mixed. \emph{Protocol~N} is the
nested design just described (configurations A, B and C at 16, 34 and 51 held-out classes),
and it carries every headline result. \emph{Protocol~P} is an earlier pilot on a single held-out
pool, Coffee, Orange and Peach, 17 classes over 1{,}618 images at chance 5.9\%, retained because
the ten-encoder survey, the from-scratch distillation baseline and the domain-model bake-off were
run on it and have not been repeated under Protocol~N. Protocol-P numbers are never compared with
Protocol-N numbers, and each is labelled at the point of use together with the descriptor strategy
it was measured under, since the pilot was scored under both the \emph{crude} and the \emph{rich}
strategy at different times.

\subsection{Source-grounded descriptors}
\label{sec:desc-gen}

Each disease carries a symptom record with fields for pathogen, affected organs and visual symptoms,
each field carrying a value, a source URL and a verbatim quote. Records are generated by Claude Sonnet 4.6 (\texttt{anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-6}) at temperature 0 with a 2{,}000-token response budget, under a strict grounding prompt that forbids un-cited claims, then audited. The full
prompt, the decoding parameters and the seven generation seeds used by the control arm of
Section~\ref{sec:strategies} are released with the descriptor registry, so the registry can be
regenerated end to end. Because the generation endpoint
cannot browse, raw citations are model-recalled; we therefore web-verify the headline held-out
diseases, replacing citations with sentences copied verbatim from reachable authoritative pages. This verification is partial and we report its extent rather than implying completeness: of the
47 filled records on held-out crops, 23 carry at least one page-verified field, and
Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives the full breakdown. The
symptom text (the string that becomes the CLIP text prototype) is the functional component; the
verified citations provide the auditability guarantee. Records that could not be grounded are stored as
explicit stubs and are excluded by the loader, so a placeholder can never become a prototype.

\subsection{Architecture}

One frozen image--text encoder per tier, plus: (1)~a trained seen-crop linear probe; (2)~a zero-shot
descriptor head selecting the nearest source-grounded text prototype by cosine similarity;
(3)~WiSE-FT weight ensembling, interpolating fine-tuned and frozen visual weights at ratio $\alpha$;
and (4)~an abstain router on the top-1 minus top-2 similarity margin. The router gates \emph{within}
whichever head is answering; it does not choose between the two heads, and nothing in this article
does. Only the image encoder deploys.
We evaluate four deployable tiers plus a reference ceiling, each pinned to one public
\texttt{open\_clip} checkpoint: MobileCLIP2-S0/\texttt{dfndr2b} (11.4\,M),
MobileCLIP-S1/\texttt{datacompdr} (21.5\,M), MobileCLIP2-S2/\texttt{dfndr2b} (35.8\,M),
MobileCLIP-B/\texttt{datacompdr} (86.3\,M) and ViT-B-16-SigLIP2/\texttt{webli} (92.9\,M, reference
only). Parameter counts are image-tower only. Because Section~\ref{sec:flat} attributes an
8.5-point spread to pretraining recipe rather than to capacity, the pretraining tag is part of the
model identity throughout and is carried in every released result file.

We compare four prompt strategies of increasing specificity, all evaluated with the same frozen
encoders and the same held-out splits. \emph{Bare} uses the class name alone. \emph{Crude} adds one
generic keyword sentence. \emph{Rich} retrieves a hand-curated symptom paragraph from a 32-key
keyword bank. \emph{Grounded} uses the LLM-authored, source-cited symptom text of
Section~\ref{sec:desc-gen}, falling back to \emph{rich} for any class without a record.

\paragraph{Why \emph{rich} cannot isolate grounding} Comparing \emph{grounded} against \emph{rich}
conflates two different properties. Retrieval assigns text by matching a disease name against 32
generic keys, so distinct classes routinely receive identical prototypes: at configuration C only 8
of the 51 held-out classes receive a unique entry, 17 fall back to the bare class name, and 26 share
text with another class, the largest groups being seven classes keyed on \emph{spot} and five on
\emph{blight}. Collisions grow with the label space (2, 14 and 26 classes at A, B and C) while
coverage stays flat (68.8\%, 61.8\%, 66.7\%). Any advantage over this baseline therefore measures
per-class \emph{distinctness} rather than sourcing. This is a limitation of symptom-bank retrieval as
we implemented it, not of per-class generation methods such as DCLIP or CuPL, which emit text per
class and cannot collide.

\paragraph{The ungrounded control} To isolate the sourcing constraint we generate a matched
\emph{ungrounded} arm: the same model, the same schema, the same temperature and the same seeds as
\emph{grounded}, with only the requirement to cite a retrievable source removed. Because the two arms
differ in exactly one instruction, the difference between them estimates the effect of sourcing
alone. Both control arms are generated by Claude Sonnet 5 at temperature 1 with a per-seed sampling
seed, a newer model and a higher temperature than the shipped registry of
Section~\ref{sec:desc-gen}, which used Claude Sonnet 4.6 at temperature 0. That is deliberate:
sampling must be stochastic for seeds to differ at all, and the grounded side of the control is
regenerated under the new settings rather than reused from the shipped registry, so the comparison
cannot confound the sourcing instruction with a change of model or decoding temperature.

\paragraph{Paired-subset evaluation} Coverage differs between the arms, and a naive comparison would
absorb that difference. Since \texttt{text\_for} falls back to the keyword bank wherever an arm has
no record, an arm covering 41 of 51 classes is four-fifths its own text and one-fifth shared bank
text, which dilutes both arms toward a common baseline. We therefore evaluate both arms on the intersection of classes that every seed of both arms filled genuinely, so that the sourcing requirement is the only difference between the texts being compared. The intersection is smaller than the full held-out set and chance rises accordingly; we report both, and we treat the full set as the primary estimate. The reason is that the intersection is not selected independently of the arms: every one of the ten excluded classes was excluded because the \emph{grounded} arm refused it, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} shows refusal tracks how well sourced a disease is. Conditioning on where grounded generation succeeded therefore selects classes on which it is most likely to do well, so the paired figure is the optimistic reading of the two and the full-set figure the unbiased one. %% REWRITTEN 2026-09-09. seed-to-seed standard deviation of 2.4 points and seven seeds, the paired interval spans

sourcing penalty as large as 0.5 points on the full set and 0.2 points on the paired subset, and
exclude only penalties larger than that. A genuine equivalence claim would need a margin fixed in advance and a two-one-sided
test against it; we did not pre-specify one, so we report the null and the intervals and
leave the equivalence question open. At 80\% power roughly eleven seeds would resolve an
effect of this size, which is the experiment we name as the way to settle it.

\subsection{The de-duplicated re-run}
\label{sec:dedup}

The held-out label set contains alias pairs and non-disease labels, catalogued in
Section~\ref{sec:limits}. Their density falls as the label space grows. Counting only the classes caught in an alias pair, 6 of 16 evaluated classes at configuration A, 8 of 34 at B and 10 of 51 at C (37.5\%, 23.5\% and 19.6\%); counting the non-disease labels as well, which enter from configuration~B because all four are Wheat labels, 6 of 16, 12 of 34 and 14 of 51 (37.5\%, 35.3\% and 27.5\%). The direction is the same either way, and it is the same direction as the apparent rise in source-grounded accuracy. A sceptical reading of
Section~\ref{sec:desc} is therefore available: the label space may be getting cleaner rather than
the method getting relatively better. We re-ran all three configurations on a cleaned label set to
separate the two, released as \texttt{zeroshot\_eval\_\{A,B,C\}\_clean.json}.

Removing one label from each of the five alias pairs and four of the five non-disease labels leaves
13, 26 and 42 classes, and raises the source-grounded mean to 29.3\%, 32.8\% and 30.8\% against the
uncleaned 21.5\%, 22.7\% and 23.9\%. Part of each rise is mechanical, since dropping classes lifts
the chance floor: at configuration C from 1.96\% to 2.38\%, so relative to chance the cleaned
figure is 12.9$\times$ against 12.2$\times$. Cleaning helps every strategy, so reporting the
uncleaned numbers as the headline remains the conservative choice.

The re-run answers the question this leaves open, and it answers it against us on one count of
three. Source-grounded accuracy rises monotonically only under the uncleaned label set with
image-weighted averaging; de-duplicated it goes 29.3\%, 32.8\%, 30.8\%, and under class-macro
averaging it falls in both label sets. \textbf{We therefore withdraw any claim that source-grounded
descriptors improve as the label space grows.} What the re-run leaves standing is the comparison:
the keyword bank falls monotonically in all four combinations of label set and averaging
convention, by between 6.0 and 12.5 points, source-grounded text does not, and it is the strongest
of the four strategies at the largest scale in all four, leading the bank by 2.4 points as
published and by 4.9 points de-duplicated and class-averaged. One further asymmetry is worth
stating because it runs in our favour and we did not look for it: under class-macro averaging the
bank's advantage at the two smaller scales nearly disappears (0.2 and 1.2 points uncleaned, 0.7 and
0.4 de-duplicated), so the lead it holds under image-weighted averaging is concentrated in a few
large classes.

\subsection{INT8 is architecture-dependent, not universally beneficial}
\label{sec:int8}

A naive dynamic-quantisation call, the default recipe in most tutorials, makes the lightweight
tiers 18--19$\times$ \emph{slower}. A properly configured static quantise--dequantise (QDQ) pipeline
(shape-inference
pre-pass, per-channel weights, calibrated activations) recovers 5.1--9.4$\times$ of that but still does not
beat FP32 on those tiers. The pure-transformer model behaves oppositely and becomes 2.2$\times$ faster.

The mechanism is visible in the quantised graphs: counting convolutions the quantiser could not
convert gives 119, 215 and 235 for the three hybrid tiers against 3 for the pure transformer. On the
hybrids the runtime must dequantise, run a float convolution, and requantise hundreds of times per
inference; dynamic quantisation instead maps every convolution to an integer-convolution operator that
is pathologically slow for depthwise kernels on x86.

\paragraph{Practical guidance} For hybrid convolution--transformer encoders on a CPU runtime, INT8 is a
\emph{size} lever, not a \emph{speed} lever; deploy FP32. That holds on all three hybrid tiers we
measured. The one pure-transformer encoder in Table~\ref{tab:edge} behaves oppositely, and the convolution counts above explain why, but two limits apply. A single row is not enough to state it as a rule; and that row is also the largest model in the table at 86.3\,M against 11.4--35.8\,M for the hybrids, so architecture and scale are not separated by this comparison. The convolution counts are what make architecture the likelier cause. Confirming it needs a pure transformer inside the hybrids' size range, or a hybrid near 86\,M: benchmarking ViT-B-16-SigLIP2, already in this study as a reference ceiling, would add a second transformer row but at 92.9\,M would leave the confound in place. We also flag rather than assume that Arm processors,
whose vector extension provides well-optimised INT8 depthwise kernels, may reverse the hybrid
result.

\section{Discussion}

%% QUALIFIED 2026-09-11. "The contribution is a system" claims more than the paper measures:
%% the two heads are evaluated separately and nothing selects between them, so what is
%% demonstrated is a set of components, not an end-to-end system. Also "flat with size" is
%% softened to match the narrowed claim in Section 5.1.
\paragraph{The contribution is the components, not the backbone} The tiers are off-the-shelf
encoders used frozen; the novelty is the source-grounded descriptor head, the margin-ranked abstain
gate, the WiSE-FT knob, and the real-time edge packaging. We measure these components
individually and do not claim an end-to-end system: selecting between the seen and unseen heads is
left open (Section~\ref{sec:limits}), so what we report is the deployable parts and their costs
rather than a fielded pipeline. Because the size effect is small relative to what size costs, the
reason to offer a family is the measured latency/size Pareto, not accuracy.

\paragraph{Source-grounding is a trust contribution, and the authoring route that scales} Grounded
generation requires one retrievable source per class, where hand-curation requires an expert per crop
and, in the retrieval form used here, cannot even keep prototypes distinct as the label space grows.
That asymmetry is what we consider the paper's transferable claim beyond agriculture, and the
ungrounded control in Section~\ref{sec:desc} settles the question it raises: the sourcing constraint
does not measurably cost accuracy. The point estimate is positive on every encoder and at both class
sets ($+1.96$ points on the paired classes and $+0.94$ on the full held-out set), but the
seed-level intervals include zero, so we claim the absence of a penalty rather than a gain. The practical reading is not that grounding is an accuracy technique --- two
points would be a thin reason to adopt anything, but that the trade-off practitioners might expect
to face between auditability and performance does not appear in our measurements. Where accuracy \emph{does} move is descriptor authoring as a whole, though only at the smallest label space is it the largest lever available. Replacing a class name with a full symptom description is worth 8.8 points at configuration~A, four times the sourcing effect, but 2.4 and 4.8 points at B and C; over the same configurations, moving from the 11.4\,M tier to the 86.3\,M one under source-grounded text is worth 8.6, 4.8 and 5.6 points. Authoring therefore edges out encoder choice at A and trails it at both B and C, and the honest ordering is that the two are comparable levers across this range rather than that one of them is \emph{the} lever. What distinguishes them is cost: the authoring gain is paid once, offline, in generation tokens, whereas the 5.6 points at C cost 5.8$\times$ the on-device latency and 7.5$\times$ the footprint (Table~\ref{tab:edge}). That asymmetry, not the point difference, is the reason to spend effort on descriptors. Sourcing is what makes either lever safe to pull in a domain where a wrong answer costs a harvest.

\paragraph{Honesty on absolutes} Fine-grained cross-crop top-1 is modest (14--29\%); we lead with top-5
and the margin-ranked abstain gate as the field-relevant metrics, and frame cross-crop transfer as a hard
open problem we advance at edge scale rather than claim to solve.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Descriptor coverage and two tiers of citation} Across 18 crops, 156 of 217 disease records
are LLM-filled; the remaining 61 are explicit stubs that fall back to the hand-curated bank. Of the
156, 112 carry a verbatim quote. We distinguish two tiers of provenance. A quote is
\emph{page-verified} when we retrieved the cited page and read the sentence there; it is
\emph{model-recalled} when the generating model supplied both quote and URL without browsing. Forty
records carry at least one page-verified field, and in 16 of those every cited field is
page-verified. Verification was prioritised by where it matters: of the 47 filled records on
held-out crops, the set carrying this paper's cross-crop claim, 23 are verified. Model-recalled
quotes are reported as unverified provenance rather than as evidence, and the per-URL audit trail is
released with the data so any citation can be checked independently.

\paragraph{The grounding prompt over-refuses} Because the generation endpoint cannot browse, a strict
``never cite what you cannot quote'' instruction makes the model return empty records rather than risk
fabrication, including for well-documented diseases. A bulk re-run converted only 1 of the 61 stubs. This
is the anti-hallucination guarantee behaving correctly, but it means grounded coverage scales with
human-supplied sources, not with more API spend.

\paragraph{Label noise in the dataset} Several held-out ``diseases'' are not diseases: three are
breeding resistance ratings, for which no symptom descriptor can exist; one is an insect pest rather
than a pathogen; and one is a misspelling of a genus. A second defect is duplication: five pairs of
held-out labels name the same disease twice: \texttt{Orange/Canker} and
\texttt{Orange/Citrus\_Canker}, \texttt{Orange/Greening\_Disease} and
\texttt{Orange/Huanglongbing}, \texttt{Peach/Leaf\_Curl} and \texttt{Peach/Peach\_Leaf\_Curl},
\texttt{Cucumber/Angular\_Leaf\_Spot} and \texttt{Cucumber/Angular\_Leaf\_Spot\_Of\_Cucumber}, and
\texttt{Wheat/Head\_Scab} and \texttt{Wheat/Fusarium\_Graminearum\_Schwabe}. No descriptor can
separate a synonymous pair, so each such class carries an accuracy ceiling near 50\%.

This bears on the scaling result specifically, and we state the risk rather than leave it to be
found: duplicate density falls as the label space grows, which is the same direction as the
apparent rise in grounded accuracy, so the two cannot be separated by the uncleaned runs alone.
Section~\ref{sec:dedup} reports the de-duplicated re-run that addresses this and the claim we
withdrew as a result.

%% ungrounded and 35/40 matched-grounded prototypes hit the 77-token ceiling. Both arms keep visual
%% vocabulary in the surviving head (51/51 and 38/40), so the comparison is not destroyed -- but a

\paragraph{Descriptor length exceeds the text context} The encoders inherit CLIP's 77-token text
window, and our generated descriptions are longer than it: essentially every generated prototype is
truncated, and the ungrounded arm, whose descriptions are the longer of the two, loses proportionally
more of its text. The truncated portions retain visual symptom vocabulary in almost every case, so
the comparison remains meaningful, but it is a comparison between the leading sentences of each
description rather than between the records as authored. A descriptor protocol written to fit the
context window (visual symptoms first, taxonomy last, or a summarisation pass before embedding)
is the obvious next refinement, and we expect it to raise both generated arms rather than to
separate them.

\paragraph{Scope and variance} Configuration C covers 18 of the dataset's crops, not all of it. %% CORRECTED 2026-09-09. This said we report no variance estimates while Section 5.3
%% reports seven seeds and a seed-level interval, and the 0.15-point reproducibility

The zero-shot, probe, abstention and supervised results are
single-seed on a single evaluation machine, and we report no variance estimates for them.
%% CORRECTED 2026-09-11. This called the control arm "the analysis our central claim rests on",
%% but the claim the abstract and conclusion actually carry is Table 2's, which the control arm
%% does not cover: the control was generated with Claude Sonnet 5 at temperature 1, the shipped

The control arm of Section~\ref{sec:desc} is the exception: it runs seven generation
seeds per arm and we report the seed-level interval there. That interval is the only variance estimate in the article, and it is measured on a separately generated registry (Claude Sonnet 5 at temperature 1, against the shipped registry's Claude Sonnet 4.6 at temperature 0), so it bounds the order of magnitude of descriptor-generation noise without formally applying to Table~\ref{tab:scale}. We nevertheless use it as the yardstick for every margin in that table, our own included, because no better one exists; this paragraph, not Section~\ref{sec:dedup}, is the authoritative statement of what it licenses. Its likely direction is worth naming because it runs in our favour: sampling at temperature 1 should be noisier than the shipped registry's temperature 0, so 2.4 points probably overstates the generation noise in Table~\ref{tab:scale} and the margins there may be firmer than we treat them. The per-crop breakdown of Table~\ref{tab:loco} carries bootstrap intervals over images. Two further records speak to determinism rather than to variance, and we separate them here so they are not read as error bars. A configuration-C re-measurement on an independently rebuilt embedding cache three weeks later reproduced each encoder to within 0.6 percentage points, and the de-duplication re-run of Section~\ref{sec:dedup}, executed in a different session on a different machine four months after the original sweep, reproduced all twenty cells of each configuration in Table~\ref{tab:scale} exactly, giving source-grounded means of 21.51\%, 22.70\% and 23.94\% against the published 21.5\%, 22.7\% and 23.9\%. Because the descriptor head is training-free and the encoders are frozen, exact reproduction is the expected outcome: it shows the pipeline is deterministic and the released files regenerate the article, and it places no bound at all on how far a margin would move under a fresh registry or a fresh split. Both records are released rather than summarised by a single agreement figure.

\paragraph{Baselines we did not run} Three comparisons a reader may reasonably expect are absent.
We do not implement per-class descriptor generation in the DCLIP~\citep{menon2023} or
CuPL~\citep{pratt2023} form, so we have no comparison against the established
descriptor-authoring methods and our evidence that per-class text avoids the bank's collisions is
indirect: the ungrounded control arm is per-class-generated and serves that role, but it was not
designed as a reimplementation of either method. Every prototype in this article is already
ensembled over three prompt templates rather than embedded from a single one, but we do not sweep
the 80-template ImageNet set that the original CLIP zero-shot recipe uses, so the bare-class-name
row is a weaker ensemble than the strongest available. And we do not report the SAGE cloud agent's accuracy on our
held-out split, so the ``deployable edge counterpart'' framing is an argument about cost and
connectivity rather than a measured accuracy comparison. Each is a run rather than a
reinterpretation, and we name them as the first additions to this benchmark.

%% convert this paper's directional claims into measured ones. They were scattered across the
%% text as asides or missing entirely; collecting them here makes the boundary of the evidence
%% explicit and gives a reviewer a concrete list rather than a general disclaimer.
\paragraph{The five runs that would settle what we report directionally} We state these together
because each corresponds to a claim we have deliberately kept directional. \emph{One.} The
descriptor registry is generated once and every cell of Table~\ref{tab:scale} is that single draw;
regenerating it over at least three seeds at all three configurations, and reporting the table as a
mean with a spread, would show whether the 2.4-point lead at 51 classes is an effect or a sample.
\emph{Two.} The ungrounded control arm is the only non-colliding comparator in the study and it
exists only at configuration C; running it at A and B would turn the scale comparison into one
between two per-class generation methods instead of one against a collision-limited bank.
\emph{Three.} Scoring one fixed image set against label spaces of 16, 34 and 51 candidates would
isolate label-space size from the crop mix, which the nested design does not do
(Section~\ref{sec:data}); it needs no new data, no new descriptors and no new training.
\emph{Four.} Nothing here selects between the seen and unseen heads. Nothing in the pipeline infers which head applies: the Introduction takes that signal as supplied by the operator, and no component recovers it when it is not. The probe has no output unit outside its 166 classes, and the descriptor head reaches only 9.3\% on those same seen classes, so mis-routing is costly in both directions; an open-set gate~\citep{scheirer2013openset} with its error rate and the resulting
end-to-end accuracy is the experiment that would make this a system rather than two components.
\emph{Five.} Benchmarking a second pure-transformer encoder under INT8 would establish the
quantisation contrast of Section~\ref{sec:int8} as a rule rather than a single observation.

\paragraph{Sub-10\,M gap} Compact aligned models below about 11\,M do exist (TinyCLIP~\citep{wu2023} ships an
8\,M image tower), but none we could load reached usable cross-crop accuracy under a
descriptor protocol, and we did not evaluate them systematically; a
$\sim$5\,M tier would require weight-inherited distillation and is framed as future work.

\section{Conclusion}

%% REWRITTEN 2026-09-11 to match the audited claims. Three changes: the headline multiple now
%% leads with the majority-class prior rather than uniform chance (the introduction already

%% marginal cost; and "model size is a near-non-factor" is narrowed to the range and the
%% cost-relative comparison that Section 5.1 actually supports.
A single frozen, compact, descriptor-driven VLM brings cross-crop plant-disease diagnosis to laptops
and small devices at no marginal cost per image, once the descriptor registry has been authored:
cross-crop zero-shot at 1.5--5.7$\times$ the majority-class prior across the four deployable tiers,
on 16--51 unseen classes, 82.2\% on 166 known classes (89.3\% for the strongest supervised CNN),
grounded top-5 of 60--77\% at 16 unseen classes and 59--75\% at 51 against random shortlists of
31.3\% and 9.8\%, a margin-ranked abstain gate,
%% Same conflation as the abstract, corrected: 17.4 ms is the FP32 build, 12.9 MB the INT8 one.
and 17.4\,ms real-time CPU inference at 45.8\,MB, compressible to 12.9\,MB under INT8 if size
outweighs latency. Across the 11.4--86.3\,M deployable family, size buys 4.8 to 8.6 points of unseen accuracy, which is real but costs up to 5.8$\times$ the latency and 7.5$\times$ the footprint to obtain; on the wider 11.4--321.8\,M pilot survey we detect no association between capacity and accuracy at all, though with ten encoders that survey could not resolve one. The levers are therefore comparable in size and differ in price: descriptor authoring, worth 8.8 points over a bare class name at the focused scale and 2.4 to 4.8 points at the wider ones, is paid once and offline, and source-grounded text is the one strategy that does not decay as the label space triples, though a de-duplicated re-run shows it does not improve either; then honest abstention; then the frozen-backbone hybrid.
What we have not shown is that these compose into a fielded system: the two heads are measured
separately, and choosing between them is left open.
